### 2. Multi-Jurisdictional Statutory Boundaries & Liability Analysis

#### A. Republic of India
- **Information Technology Act, 2000 & 2021 Intermediary Rules (2026 Consolidated Amendments):**
  - Section 79 provides conditional safe-harbor protection to intermediaries. However, under Rule 3(2)(b), this protection is strictly conditioned upon removing or disabling access to non-consensual intimate imagery or sexually explicit material within **two hours** of receiving actual knowledge or a victim complaint.
  - Model repositories that fail to implement accessible grievance officer escalation or ignore verified Section 79 takedown notices risk losing safe harbor, exposing platform operators to criminal prosecution under Sections 66E, 67, and 67A.
- **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012:**
  - Sections 13, 14, and 15 strictly prohibit the creation, storage, or distribution of synthetic child sexual abuse material. Section 19 imposes mandatory reporting requirements on institutions and administrators.
- **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:**
  - Section 79 criminalizes acts intended to insult the modesty of a woman, while Section 351 applies to extortion schemes involving synthetic media.

#### B. United States
- **Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act (47 U.S.C. § 230):**
  - While Section 230 shields interactive computer services from civil liability for third-party user content, it provides **zero immunity** against federal criminal statutes (18 U.S.C. §§ 2252, 2252A - CSAM) or intellectual property infringements.
  - Emerging federal statutory initiatives (including the TAKE IT DOWN Act and the DEFIANCE Act) establish private rights of action and strict civil liability for knowingly hosting or distributing non-consensual intimate deepfakes.

#### C. United Kingdom
- **Online Safety Act 2023 & Crime and Policing Act 2026:**
  - Intimate image abuse is classified as a statutory "priority illegal offense." Regulated services must proactively design safety architectures to prevent users from encountering or circulating NCII.
  - Ofcom holds statutory enforcement powers, including fines up to 10% of global qualifying revenue or business disruption orders against non-compliant entities.

#### D. European Union
- **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689):**
  - Article 50 imposes transparency obligations requiring providers and deployers of AI systems generating deepfakes to mark and label outputs in a machine-readable format.
  - High-risk AI classifications apply to systems used for biometric identification or law enforcement evaluation.
- **Digital Services Act (DSA):**
  - Requires Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) to conduct systemic risk assessments regarding gender-based violence and child online safety.

#### E. Australia
- **Online Safety Act 2021 (Amended 2024/2026):**
  - The Australian eSafety Commissioner operates an adult cyber abuse and image-based abuse scheme with extraterritorial jurisdiction. The Commissioner can issue legally binding removal notices with 24-hour compliance deadlines to overseas model repositories and hosts, backed by civil penalties exceeding AUD 500,000.

### 3. Ethical Safeguards & Harm Minimization Protocol

In recognition of the extreme sensitivity of synthetic sexual abuse material:
1. **Zero Exploitation Repository:** This project does not store, host, mirror, or link to any child sexual abuse material (CSAM), non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII), or operational unmoderated model checkpoints.
2. **Aggregated Reporting:** All technical and quantitative evaluations are reported exclusively at the aggregate architectural level (e.g., parameter distributions, licensing classifications, missing safety cards).
3. **No Operational Evasion Guides:** No prompt engineering guides, bypass jailbreaks, or unmoderated generation pipelines are published.

### 4. Legal Recourse Limitations & Intermediary Jurisdictional Challenges

Statutory enforcement across decentralized technology supply chains faces notable legal hurdles:
- **Jurisdictional Arbitrage:** Operators of commercial deepfake portals frequently host servers in non-extradition jurisdictions or behind bulletproof reverse proxies, complicating service of process.
- **Anonymity of Model Uploaders:** Model repositories permitting anonymous pseudonymous uploads complicate civil discovery against primary creators.
- **Dual-Use Legal Protections:** Open-source licenses (MIT, Apache 2.0) contain broad disclaimer clauses disclaiming liability for downstream consequential damages. Policy interventions must target upstream platform distribution conduct rather than purely copyright assignment.
