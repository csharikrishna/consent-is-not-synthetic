Technology / Research context

Understand the system. Address the risk.

Model architecture, distribution, synthetic identities and digital forensics are distinct parts of the problem. Each requires different safeguards.

Models are not all the same

A foundation model, derivative checkpoint, adapter and inference application have different technical roles. The original research distinguishes them to avoid inflated model counts.

Foundation models

Learned weights underpin image synthesis. Latent diffusion is one architectural approach.

[11] Rombach et al. ↗

Adaptations and components

Fine-tuning and adapters change or augment learned behavior; quantization changes representation precision. They are not independent architectures.

[16] Hu et al. ↗

Distribution and deployment

Publishers, repositories and hosting operators have different opportunities to address risk. Access alone is not evidence of misuse.

[69] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗

Dataset provenance

Publisher reports describe dataset withdrawal and remediation after potentially illegal links were identified. This is not proof about every downstream model.

[8] LAION ↗
Read the evidence carefully.
No model capability tests were conducted. The public project does not provide installation, fine-tuning, bypass or acquisition instructions for abuse-oriented tools. The aggregate metadata audit is available on the Methodology page.

Where synthetic identity abuse appears

Threat evidence

Harassment and extortion

Official advisories describe manipulated imagery being used in harassment and sexual extortion.

[21] FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center ↗[22] Financial Crimes Enforcement Network ↗
Selected dataset

1,420 accounts

A research dataset of X/Twitter accounts with GAN-generated profile photos documented inauthentic uses. It is not representative of all accounts.

[25] Journal of Online Trust and Safety ↗
Platform enforcement

Coordinated inauthentic behavior

Meta described likely synthetic profile imagery in disrupted networks. A network-specific enforcement finding is not nationwide prevalence.

[23] Meta ↗[24] Meta ↗

Distinguish fictional personas from deception.

Disclosed fictional characters and legitimate creative use are not the same as impersonation, fraud or abusive sexualization. Truthful endorsements and disclosure matter.

[31] Federal Trade Commission ↗

Forensics: complementary signals

A detector score alone cannot establish that a real person consented, that an image is authentic, or that a crime occurred.

01

Provenance

Check available signed credentials and source history.

02

Detection

Use evaluated signals with uncertainty and error rates.

03

Context

Corroborate with authorized, lawful investigation.

04

Review

Preserve due process and support survivors.

[32] NIST ↗[33] Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity ↗[34] Zhu et al. ↗
Read the evidence carefully.
Content Credentials can document signed assertions; they do not certify consent or factual truth. Research benchmark accuracy is not universal production accuracy. Never obtain or redistribute suspected illegal media to test a detector.

What intervention can and cannot establish

Observed

Regulator-reported downloadable-model misuse, repository policy changes and service access or safeguard changes.

[69] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗[70] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗

Not established

Model-specific victimization prevalence, universal model danger, causally measured harm reduction from the interventions, or evidence that all open-weight releases should be prohibited.
