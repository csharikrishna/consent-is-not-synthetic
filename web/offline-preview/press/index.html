3. Ethical & Responsible Reporting Guidelines

Media reporting on synthetic sexual abuse directly affects victim recovery, social stigma, and public understanding. We urge newsrooms to adhere to trauma-informed reporting standards:

Recommended Reporting Practices

  • Focus on Perpetrator Responsibility: Frame abuse as intentional violations of consent rather than an unavoidable consequence of technology.
  • Highlight Support Avenues: Always append verified reporting and support hotlines (e.g., Take It Down, StopNCII, National Cyber Crime portals) to articles.
  • Contextualize Statistics: Specify survey boundaries (e.g., UNICEF’s 1.2M is across 11 surveyed nations, not an additive global total).
  • Respect Survivor Autonomy: Never name non-consenting victims or disclose identifying details without explicit, uncoerced consent.

Harmful Practices to Avoid

  • Do NOT Publish Tool Names or URLs: Never name, link, or provide search terms for active commercial undressing websites or unmoderated LoRAs.
  • Do NOT Reproduce Blurred / Pixelated Abuse Media: Even blurred depictions amplify dignitary harm and re-traumatize victims.
  • Do NOT Frame Abuse as ‘Pranks’: Avoid trivializing terminology such as “schoolboy pranks” or “mischief”; use accurate statutory definitions.
  • Do NOT Conflate Synthetic with Harmless: Avoid portraying synthetic imagery as “victimless” or less harmful than physical photography.
