Platform & Supply-Chain Accountability

Model Publisher & Repository Governance

Establishing proportionate obligations across model developers, fine-tuners, and hosting repositories to prevent abuse while protecting legitimate research.

The Generative AI Supply Chain

Intervention must target the specific tier of the technology stack where risk is introduced, rather than treating all software identically.

Tier 01

Foundation Developers

Creators of base pretrained models (e.g., Latent Diffusion, SDXL, FLUX, Qwen). Responsibilities include dataset screening, documentation of unlearned safety concepts, and clear license restrictions.

[11] Rombach et al. ↗[12] Podell et al. ↗[13] Black Forest Labs ↗
Tier 02

Fine-Tuners & Adapters

Community actors creating LoRA adapters and fine-tunes. Lightweight adapters (150 MB–700 MB) can alter base model representations to bypass safety boundaries or specialize in uncensored depiction.

[16] Hu et al. ↗[18] Ruiz et al. ↗
Tier 03

Repositories & Hubs

Model-sharing platforms (e.g., Hugging Face, Civitai). Critical responsibilities include enforcing terms against purpose-built abuse weights, maintaining abuse reporting intake, and cooperating with regulatory inquiries.

[9] Hugging Face ↗[10] Hugging Face ↗[69] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗

Key Governance Pillars

A defensible governance framework requires verifiable transparency rather than superficial marketing claims.

1. Standardized Safety Documentation

Model cards must record training dataset provenance, declared parameter counts, intended use domains, known failure modes, and safety evaluations. In our empirical audit of 53 historical model records, zero models had published safety evaluation reports.

Review Completeness Audit↗

2. Enforceable Acceptable-Use Licenses

Open licenses must incorporate enforceable downstream restrictions against non-consensual intimate depiction and child sexual exploitation (e.g., CreativeML-OpenRAIL terms). Repositories must treat license violations as platform terms violations.

[9] Hugging Face ↗[13] Black Forest Labs ↗

3. Pre-Release Independent Risk Holds

When an artifact demonstrates critical, unmitigated residual risks of producing illegal material or automated sexual impersonation, developers and distributors must implement a proportionate release hold pending third-party audit.

[63] US National Telecommunications and Information Administration ↗[69] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗

4. Responsive Repository Moderation

Repositories must deploy proactive screening for known abuse tags, maintain accessible complaint mechanisms, and act swiftly upon substantiated reports. Australia’s eSafety engagement demonstrated that host policy changes are technically and operationally feasible.

[69] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗[70] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗

Fairness & Precision in Model Evaluation

Read the evidence carefully.
Do Not Conflate Open Weights with Inherent Danger: Open-source and open-weight models drive academic security research, competitive innovation, and transparency. A model should never be declared “criminal” or “dangerous” solely because it is open-weight or community-developed. Governance must target documented harmful conduct and purpose-built exploitation tools.
