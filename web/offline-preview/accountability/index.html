The Three-Tier Supply Chain Framework

A principled public-interest model distinguishing intentional abuse from general-purpose research.

Tier 01 / Prohibit

Purpose-Built Abuse Tools

Strict civil and criminal prohibitions on the commercial development, hosting, and supply of software, websites, and fine-tunes designed or adapted specifically to generate non-consensual sexual imagery or CSAM.

Tier 02 / Restrict & Screen

Unresolved Supply-Chain Risk

Mandate independent safety assessments, verified publisher identity, and automated pre-upload heuristic screening for high-capacity open-weight models and repository hosting hubs.

Tier 03 / Permit & Protect

Legitimate Research & Creative Use

Establish clear legal safe harbors for academic red-teaming, computer vision benchmarking, consensual artistic expression, and legitimate safety evaluations under accredited ethical oversight.

Stakeholder Group 01Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

AI Model Developers & Laboratories

Documented Problem & Evidence

Foundation models released with unconstrained open weights are downstream fine-tuned using parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA) to remove safety alignment and generate photorealistic non-consensual imagery.

Empirical Grounding: 722 open model repositories audited; 68% are lightweight adapters specialized on top of general foundation releases <Source id="11"/> <Source id="12"/> <Source id="16"/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Implement architectural defense-in-depth: negative prompt conditioning baked into core latent spaces, immutable safety fine-tuning that resists low-rank inversion, and pre-release red-teaming on adversarial personalization.

Implementation Mechanism: Contractual terms in open-weight model licenses (e.g., OpenRAIL-M) with enforceable termination clauses, and publishing cryptographic model card benchmarks evaluating degradation under adversarial fine-tuning.

Expected Outcome Metric:Percentage of downstream community LoRAs capable of generating photorealistic anatomical nudity without triggering runtime latent collapse.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Open weights executed on offline consumer GPUs cannot be remotely revoked; architectural latent guards can still face adversarial distillation over time.
Stakeholder Group 02Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Model-Hosting Repositories & Hubs

Documented Problem & Evidence

Repositories host and index thousands of specialized fine-tunes with zero upload identity verification, missing model cards, and unscrubbed training datasets.

Empirical Grounding: 81% of audited community fine-tunes omitted training data provenance; 73% lacked any model card safety warnings <Source id={9}/> <Source id={10}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Mandate verified publisher phone/email authentication, require affirmative training dataset provenance declarations, implement automated pre-upload heuristic scanning for predatory anatomical tags, and enforce hash-based takedown registers.

Implementation Mechanism: Integration of automated safety linters into model pull requests; fast-track 24-hour statutory takedown portals for child protection hotlines and affected individuals.

Expected Outcome Metric:Median platform response and removal latency for verified community abuse reports; percentage of public models with validated publisher identity.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Aggressive automated scanning may flag legitimate academic red-teaming research; requires transparent, due-process appeal pathways.
Stakeholder Group 03Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Social Media & Interactive Platforms

Documented Problem & Evidence

Commercial platforms act as viral distribution funnels for non-consensual synthetic media, while relying on slow, reactive victim reporting mechanisms.

Empirical Grounding: Over 60% of child technology-facilitated sexual exploitation cases involve mainstream social networks <Source id={54}/>; 397 reports received by UK Childline in 2025 rose to 420 in H1 2026 alone <Source id={55}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Deploy automated perceptual hash matching (integrating StopNCII and Take It Down databases), enforce strict 24-hour to 48-hour takedown windows upon receipt of verified victim notices, and terminate repeat distributor accounts.

Implementation Mechanism: API integration with global hashing consortiums; displaying prominent in-app reporting flows specifically tailored for synthetic sexual abuse.

Expected Outcome Metric:Proportion of reported synthetic NCII items removed within statutory time limits (48 hours US / 2-3 hours India / 24 hours Australia).
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Content moderation at scale faces challenges with end-to-end encrypted messaging channels (e.g. Telegram); platform takedown does not remove local source files.
Stakeholder Group 04Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Cloud GPU & Infrastructure Providers

Documented Problem & Evidence

Commercial undressing web applications and Telegram bot services rely on low-cost, on-demand serverless cloud GPU containers to execute inference at massive scale without owning physical servers.

Empirical Grounding: ISD documented 20.7 million monthly visits across 31 sampled commercial abuse websites relying on cloud GPU hosting pipelines <Source id={20}/> <Source id={66}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Implement Know Your Business (KYB) onboarding protocols for high-concurrency serverless inference instances, and maintain zero-tolerance terms prohibiting commercial deployment of non-consensual intimate imagery generation.

Implementation Mechanism: Monitoring container image hashes against known abusive undressing tool registries, and terminating serverless endpoints flagged by verified law enforcement notices.

Expected Outcome Metric:Number of commercial deepfake bot services disrupted through infrastructure-level hosting termination.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Operators may migrate between anonymous offshore cloud hosts and decentralized cryptocurrency-funded compute providers.
Stakeholder Group 05Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Government Regulators & Enforcement Agencies

Documented Problem & Evidence

Regulatory enforcement is often delayed by jurisdictional fragmentation, technical opacity, and lack of dedicated specialized cyber investigative resources.

Empirical Grounding: Ofcom found 66.35% (69 of 104) of reviewed online service risk assessment records lacked a designated responsible person <Source id={75}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Issue binding cross-stack enforcement orders, conduct proactive audits of model-hosting supply chains, and levy proportionate civil monetary penalties on platforms failing due-diligence standards.

Implementation Mechanism: Empowering specialized investigators to issue civil penalty notices directly to offshore platforms and model hubs under cross-border regulatory treaties <Source id={43}/> <Source id={71}/>.

Expected Outcome Metric:Number of formal regulatory interventions resulting in service withdrawal, geo-blocking, or verified compliance overhauls <Source id={70}/>.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Geoblocking orders can be bypassed using virtual private networks (VPNs); requires coordinated upstream supply-chain enforcement.
Stakeholder Group 06Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Legislators & Parliaments

Documented Problem & Evidence

Outdated criminal statutes historically required physical photographic capture or physical contact, creating legal loopholes for synthetic forgeries and tool distributors.

Empirical Grounding: Inconsistent legal definitions across jurisdictions; victims face years of civil ambiguity before criminal courts interpret legacy privacy laws <Source id={44}/> <Source id={45}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Enact standalone, technology-neutral criminal offenses penalizing the intentional creation, distribution, and commercial supply of non-consensual intimate synthetic depictions (regardless of whether distributed).

Implementation Mechanism: Modernizing penal codes (e.g., UK Crime & Policing Act 2026 ss. 72/99 <Source id={41}/>, Canadian Bill C-16 <Source id={44}/>, South Korean Sexual Crimes Act Art 14-2 <Source id={46}/>, US Take It Down Act <Source id={40}/>).

Expected Outcome Metric:Enactment of comprehensive statutory protections with enforceable extraterritorial reach and statutory damages provisions.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Statutory drafting must carefully define criminal intent to avoid chilling legitimate creative arts, parody, political speech, and academic safety red-teaming.
Stakeholder Group 07Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Educational Institutions & School Boards

Documented Problem & Evidence

Schools face a surge in peer-to-peer synthetic harassment and sexual deepfakes targeting students and teachers, with administrators lacking clear legal protocols.

Empirical Grounding: 3% of Australian children report having had fabricated nudes made of them <Source id={68}/>; IWF and Childline report hundreds of British youth seeking removal assistance <Source id={55}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Establish mandatory digital consent and AI literacy curricula, implement clear zero-tolerance institutional policies classifying synthetic sexual generation as sexual harassment, and provide immediate confidential referral pathways.

Implementation Mechanism: Staff training on trauma-informed student intake, safe evidence preservation without circulating CSAM, and partnership with Take It Down and national hotlines.

Expected Outcome Metric:School adoption rates of formal synthetic abuse response protocols and student awareness of anonymous hashing tools.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:Overly punitive zero-tolerance policies can discourage early disclosure; restorative and educational frameworks must accompany disciplinary measures.
Stakeholder Group 08Evidence-Grounded Recommendation

Child Protection & Safeguarding NGOs

Documented Problem & Evidence

Non-governmental victim support organizations are overwhelmed by the volume of synthetic media reports and lack resources to process complex AI cases.

Empirical Grounding: Over 1.2 million internet-using youth estimated affected across 11 surveyed nations <Source id={53}/>; NCMEC handling >400k generative AI reports <Source id={7}/>.

Proposed Specific Action & Implementation

Expand international funding for on-device hashing technologies, deliver community-level awareness campaigns dispelling the myth of &ldquo;victimless&rdquo; AI generation, and provide legal aid to survivors.

Implementation Mechanism: Deploying multilingual public awareness resources and establishing direct coordination hotlines with local police cyber cells.

Expected Outcome Metric:Average survivor intake wait times and verified takedown success rates across participating platforms.
Limitations & Boundary Caveats:NGOs rely on donor and public funding; sustainable statutory funding models are required to match commercial generative growth.

Dedicated Policy Focus: Republic of India

Analysis of India's statutory framework under the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the consolidated IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Amendment Rules, 2026.

Enacted Statutory Framework

IT Rules 2026: Expedited Response Deadlines

India has established some of the world’s most stringent takedown deadlines for synthetic intimate abuse:

  • Rule 3(2)(b) — 2-Hour Window: Intermediaries must take all reasonable and practicable measures to disable access within two hours of receiving a complaint from an individual or authorized person regarding non-consensual sexually explicit content [74] Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, India ↗.
  • Rule 3(1)(d) — 3-Hour Window: Intermediaries must remove unlawful content within three hours of receiving formal actual knowledge via court order or authorized government notification [74] Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, India ↗.
  • Rule 3(3) — Synthetic Due Diligence: Mandates clear provenance notices and technical watermarking for synthetic information generated or hosted on intermediary systems [37] MeitY ↗.
Implementation Challenges

Operational Gaps & Proposed Reforms

While statutory timelines are aggressive, significant enforcement hurdles persist in practice:

  • Offshore Supply-Chain Evasion: Foreign model hubs, decentralized torrent trackers, and anonymous Telegram bots frequently lack resident Grievance Officers in India, complicating direct rule enforcement.
  • Technical Verification Capacity: State-level cyber cells require standardized tooling to authenticate digital evidence without requiring survivors to circulate original explicit files.
  • Statutory Damages: Civil remedies under Section 43A of the IT Act should be strengthened to provide statutory minimum compensation for survivors of synthetic dignitary violations.
