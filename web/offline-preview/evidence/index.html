5. Global Regulatory Matrix: Comparative Statutory Analysis

Interactive comparison of legal frameworks across 8 major jurisdictions. Distinguishes active criminal statutes, mandatory platform takedown windows, and identified regulatory gaps.

8 jurisdictions analyzed

🇮🇳 India

In force / Amended

Primary statute: Information Technology Act (Sec 66E, 67, 67A) & IT Rules 2021 (amended Feb 2026)

Effective date:February 2026Takedown timeline:2 hours (intimate complaint) / 3 hours (actual knowledge)Statutory scope:Non-consensual intimate depictions, impersonation, synthetic generated information due diligence (Rule 3(3)).
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Take all reasonable and practicable measures within 2 hours of qualifying intimate image complaint.
  • Remove or disable access within 3 hours upon specified actual knowledge trigger.
  • Deploy grievance redressal and transparent compliance reporting under Rule 3(3).

Penalties: Revocation of intermediary safe harbor protection (Sec 79); criminal liability under Sec 67A (up to 5 years imprisonment).

Identified policy gaps: Extraterritorial enforcement on standalone open-weight weights; jurisdictional overlap with state law enforcement.

[36] Government of India, Press Information Bureau ↗[37] MeitY ↗[74] Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, India ↗

🇺🇸 United States

In force (Section 3 active)

Primary statute: TAKE IT DOWN Act & FTC Act Section 5

Effective date:19 May 2026Takedown timeline:48 hours from valid requestStatutory scope:Covered platforms must remove qualifying intimate imagery, including realistic computer-generated representations.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Remove reported intimate content and known identical copies within 48 hours of valid notice.
  • Establish clear, accessible reporting mechanisms for minors and adults.
  • Comply with FTC compliance and audit requirements.

Penalties: FTC civil enforcement, civil penalties; severe federal criminal sanctions for CSAM under 18 U.S.C. §§ 2252/2252A.

Identified policy gaps: Preemption of state deepfake civil action statutes; open-source model repositories not cleanly categorized as covered platforms.

[40] Federal Trade Commission ↗[72] US Federal Trade Commission ↗

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

Enacted / Phased Commencement

Primary statute: Crime and Policing Act 2026 & Online Safety Act 2023

Effective date:2026 (provisions subject to commencement orders)Takedown timeline:Time-bound takedown duties under Ofcom codesStatutory scope:Specific criminal offence targeting creation of sexually explicit deepfakes; broad provider illegal content duties.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Conduct illegal content risk assessments with named senior accountable owners (Ofcom compliance).
  • Take proactive, proportionate measures to prevent users encountering non-consensual intimate imagery.
  • Implement fast-track takedown and preservation workflows.

Penalties: Fines up to 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue under Online Safety Act; criminal prosecution of creators.

Identified policy gaps: Enforcement across decentralized peer-to-peer file sharing and overseas model-sharing repositories.

[41] UK Government ↗[42] Crown Prosecution Service ↗[73] Ofcom ↗[75] Ofcom ↗

🇪🇺 European Union

Phased Application

Primary statute: AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689, Art. 50) & Digital Services Act (DSA)

Effective date:2 August 2026 (Article 50)Takedown timeline:Expeditious removal under DSA notice-and-actionStatutory scope:Mandatory transparency, watermarking, and disclosure for deepfakes and generative AI media; DSA systemic risk duties for VLOPs.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Label AI-generated and manipulated audio, image, and video content in machine-readable formats.
  • Disclose that content has been artificially generated or manipulated to users.
  • Very Large Online Platforms must mitigate systemic risks related to gender-based violence and illegal content.

Penalties: Administrative fines up to €35 million or 7% of annual worldwide turnover under the AI Act.

Identified policy gaps: Susceptibility of synthetic watermarks to post-processing removal; open-weight carve-outs in earlier chapters.

[38] European Union ↗[39] European Commission ↗

🇦🇺 Australia

Active enforcement / Legislative expansion

Primary statute: Online Safety Act 2021 & Digital Duty of Care Bill 2026

Effective date:2021 / 2026 inquiry submissionTakedown timeline:24 hours from formal eSafety removal noticeStatutory scope:Image-based abuse scheme covering digitally altered, synthetic, or manipulated depictions of identifiable persons.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Comply with formal removal notices within 24 hours of notification.
  • Cooperate with regulatory investigation notices and supply-chain inquiries.
  • Model hosts must enforce terms prohibiting services that nudify Australian minors.

Penalties: Federal Court civil penalties exceeding AUD 343,500; injunctions and service blocking orders.

Identified policy gaps: Tracking user displacement when offshore websites ignore civil penalty enforcement.

[43] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗[69] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗[70] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗[71] Australian eSafety Commissioner ↗

🇨🇦 Canada

Enacted / Implementation pending

Primary statute: Bill C-16 (Protecting Victims Act, Royal Assent 18 June 2026)

Effective date:18 June 2026 (Royal Assent)Takedown timeline:Criminal justice proceedings & court-ordered injunctionsStatutory scope:Amends Criminal Code Section 162.1 to expressly include realistic synthetic representations in non-consensual intimate image offences.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Prohibits distribution, publishing, or making available non-consensual realistic intimate depictions.
  • Intermediaries subject to court-ordered removal warrants.

Penalties: Indictable criminal offence carrying up to 5 years imprisonment; peace bonds and restraining orders.

Identified policy gaps: Absence of statutory administrative deadline for non-criminal platform takedowns.

[44] Parliament of Canada ↗

🇯🇵 Japan

Case-by-case statutory application

Primary statute: Penal Code (Defamation Art. 230, Insult Art. 231) & Ministry Guidance

Effective date:Reaffirmed 2025/2026Takedown timeline:Notice-and-takedown under Provider Liability Limitation ActStatutory scope:Criminal defamation or insult depending on specific circumstances; copyright infringement for character adaptation.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Platform voluntary moderation upon receiving infringement notices.
  • Disclosure of sender identification information upon court order.

Penalties: Criminal fines and imprisonment under Penal Code; civil tort liability for violation of privacy and portrait rights.

Identified policy gaps: No dedicated statutory ban on synthetic intimate depictions where defamation cannot be formally established.

[45] House of Representatives, Japan ↗

🇰🇷 South Korea

Strict statutory enforcement

Primary statute: Act on Special Cases Concerning Punishment of Sexual Crimes (Art. 14-2)

Effective date:Active (amended 2024–2026)Takedown timeline:Immediate emergency blocking via KCSC deliberationStatutory scope:Explicitly criminalizes editing, synthesizing, processing, or distributing a person's face, body, or voice into sexual depictions without consent.
Platform obligations & penalties
  • Telecommunications business operators must filter and block reported illegal synthetic sexual materials.
  • Korea Communications Standards Commission issues expedited access restriction orders.

Penalties: Imprisonment for up to 5 to 7 years, or criminal fines up to 50 million KRW; aggravated penalties for commercial intent.

Identified policy gaps: Enforcement against encrypted messaging groups and overseas mirror accounts.

[46] Korea Law Information Center ↗
