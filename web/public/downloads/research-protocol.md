# Publication readiness and prospective research protocol
Version 1 | 25 November 2026 | AI-assisted working draft, not preregistered or independently reviewed

## Scope and status
This supplement strengthens V3 without replacing it. All 76 inherited references are indexed and provisionally classified; 17 selected reference entries have individual claims rechecked against public sources. Remaining claims, including other claims within those entries, require review. The 10-event register is purposive and illustrative, not a representative incident sample. The 53-record inventory is a seed set, not a global census. No harmful outputs, survivor data or live service tests were collected. Independent reviewers must be actual people unaffiliated with the draft's production.

## A. Systematic evidence review (prospective)
Question: What documented synthetic sexual abuse occurs, what factors enable it, and which interventions reduce availability, exposure or victim harm? Separate adults and children, altered and fully synthetic media, and observed outcomes from hypothetical capabilities. Include peer-reviewed empirical studies, official statistics, legal instruments, regulator decisions and documented cases. Exclude unsupported anecdotes, duplicate reports of one event, promotional claims without verification, and sources that only describe how to produce harmful material. Contextual technical papers and legal documents remain distinct from empirical impact studies.

Search period: 2018 to 25 November 2026; search dates and coverage to be logged. Proposed databases: ACM Digital Library, Scopus or Web of Science (if accessible), PubMed, Google Scholar, official UNICEF/IWF/NCMEC, regulators and court websites. Example concept query: (deepfake OR synthetic intimate imagery OR AI-generated abuse) AND (victimization OR prevalence OR harm OR prevention OR enforcement). Record database, exact query, filters, date, hit count, exported identifiers and access failures. The existing 76 entries are a convenience seed list, not the yield of this prospective search. No PRISMA flow counts are claimed. Use PRISMA 2020 reporting checklist (https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020-checklist) and document deviations.

Two human reviewers independently screen title/abstract then full text; resolve disagreement via a third reviewer. Deduplicate DOI/URL and study cohort; log full-text exclusions. Extract design, sample, denominator, sampling period, jurisdiction, definition, measurement, uncertainty, limitations, conflicts, and primary source locator. Rate risk of bias by design (sampling/measurement/confounding/missingness/selective reporting); report limitations by claim. Do not pool different reporting units or overlapping cohorts. Meta-analysis only if compatible outcomes and sufficient studies; otherwise structured narrative synthesis. Publish search log, exclusions and extraction data without harmful imagery or identifying details.

## B. Incident register
The accompanying CSV separates criminal sentences, verdicts, arrests, civil penalties, regulator warnings and service responses. Add case identifiers, primary court/regulator record, date of update, model attribution only if independently established, impact and outcome status. Link duplicate reporting to a single event. Do not publish victim names, images, or abuse-service links. This is not an estimate of frequency; geographical coverage reflects available source discovery. Independent human verification is required before attributing criminal acts to individuals.

## C. Legal review checklist
Counsel in each jurisdiction should verify official text, amendments, commencement dates, scope of providers, territorial nexus, definitions, defenses and research exceptions, remedies, deadlines, and enforceability. Distinguish enactment from commencement and guidance from binding law. India: check Rule 3(2)(b) and Rule 3(1)(d) separately, along with Rule 3(3), POCSO and the IT Act. UK: verify commencement of Crime and Policing Act 2026 sections 72 and 99 before making claims of operative criminal liability. US: distinguish FTC platform removal duties from criminal provisions. This is a preliminary document review, not legal advice or independent counsel sign-off.

## D. Authorized safety assessment (prospective)
Do not generate or possess abusive material, use real children's or non-consenting persons' images, probe illicit services, or circumvent safeguards. Obtain written institutional authorization, ethics/legal review, data handling and reporting procedures, and an independent safety lead. Begin with benign tests of policy disclosure, complaint intake, provenance integrity, account permissions, and moderation appeals; use consenting adults or innocuous non-person assets. Any specialized evaluation of child-safety capabilities must be performed by qualified authorized institutions under their approved protocols; use published aggregate results rather than reproducing harmful outputs. Measure detection and false positives on lawful data; document model version, sample and confidence intervals. No tests have been performed in this project.

## E. Longitudinal intervention evaluation (prospective)
Hypothesis: targeted restrictions, governance and removal duties can reduce exposure; service changes alone do not demonstrate that outcome. Choose a defined jurisdiction and intervention date; pre-register primary outcomes, exposure definition, comparator and analysis. Proposed unit: service-month or aggregate jurisdiction-month, obtained via regulator or consenting platform data. Collect monthly pre/post observations (aim for 12 months each subject to access); retain stable definitions and denominator. Primary outcome: repeat exposure per resolved eligible complaint within 30 days (report missing follow-up). Secondary: timely removal proportion, verified policy compliance, lawful appeals upheld, substitution/displacement, and victim-reported safety only through approved surveys. Report uncertainty and missingness. Interrupted time series with seasonality and autocorrelation; comparative analysis if suitable controls and pre-trends support it. Document concurrent policy changes, detection changes, and confounding; sensitivity analysis for denominator shifts. Do not claim causal reduction without supporting design. No longitudinal dataset or trial exists yet.

## F. Model metadata census (prospective)
Include ordinary general-purpose official image models and declared release variants, distinguish artifact class and parent. Freeze repository revision and capture license text, file manifest sizes, precision and component, publisher-reported parameters and published safety assessment status. Deduplicate mirrors by public revision/hash metadata where available. Never infer safety from missing reports or weight size from VRAM. Avoid collecting a directory of abuse-oriented adaptations or ranking harmful capability. The current completeness CSV quantifies populated fields in the existing 53 rows, not verified model specifications. Unknown values must remain unknown until documented.

## G. External review and release gate
Seek independent reviewers in child safety, quantitative methods, AI governance and law. Request signed/date-stamped comments and conflicts declaration; maintain a response-to-reviewers log and a correction register. Before public release check every headline figure against the linked source; disclose AI assistance and limits; remove abusive imagery, victim identifiers and operational abuse details; check intended journal preprint policy. Before journal submission complete full citation audit, screening, expert feedback and author accountability. Publish the public brief as a preliminary evidence summary only, not a peer-reviewed paper. The primary V3 manuscript remains unchanged; apply the corrections below before submission.

## H. Corrections and claim gates
- Ofcom 69/104: printed page 18, not 17. This is a selected sample of online-safety records, not a census of AI companies.
- IWF online executive summary now reports 3,443 AI-generated videos, consistent with detailed video page; an earlier conflicting figure should be described only with dated archived evidence. Other IWF publications can still differ (e.g. parent guidance lists 3,440). Use the detailed report and identify its version.
- India: two-hour requirement is for reasonable and practicable measures under qualifying individual complaints; separate three-hour provision concerns specified actual knowledge. Do not imply universal two-hour guaranteed deletion.
- UNICEF 1.2 million: weighted 11-country estimate, not worldwide. 20 million and fourfold associations concern broader technology-facilitated abuse, not AI-only causal outcomes.
- FTC warning letters, arrests, investigations, and changes to policies are distinct from final judgments or measured reductions in harm.
