## Institutional Intervention on Generative AI Synthetic Child Exploitation and Mandatory Reporting in Educational Institutions
**Addressed To:** National Commission for Protection of Child Rights (NCPCR), Government of India  
**Filing Initiative:** Consent Is Not Synthetic Research Working Group  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Safeguarding Children from Synthetic Non-Consensual Imagery under the POCSO Act, 2012  

---

### 1. Executive Summary & Child Protection Emergency

The weaponization of generative artificial intelligence to produce non-consensual intimate depictions of school students has emerged as one of the most acute child protection crises globally. 

According to UNICEF Innocenti’s 11-country digital harms study, an estimated **1.2 million children** were affected by sexual deepfake victimization within a 12-month period, with girls disproportionately targeted (41% female vs. 29% male among exposed respondents). Concurrently, the Internet Watch Foundation (IWF) assessed **8,029 realistic AI-generated child sexual abuse media files** in 2025 alone, with 54% depicting children estimated between ages 11 and 13.

In India, schools and colleges across urban and semi-urban centers have experienced incidents where students' social media photographs were scraped, altered via automated "nudify" applications or open-weight LoRA models, and circulated across messaging groups to extort, bully, and humiliate victims.

---

### 2. Statutory Analysis under the POCSO Act, 2012

Under the **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012**:
- **Section 13 (Use of Child for Pornographic Purposes):** Any person who uses a child or the likeness of a child in any form for sexual depiction or representation commits an offense punishable by rigorous imprisonment.
- **Section 14 & 15 (Punishment for Storage of Child Pornography):** The storage, possession, or distribution of synthetic child sexual abuse material constitutes a severe cognizable offense.
- **Section 19 (Mandatory Reporting Requirement):** Any person, including school principals, teachers, or administrators, who has knowledge that an offense under the POCSO Act has been committed has a strict **statutory legal duty** to report the matter immediately to the Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU) or local police. Failure to report constitutes an offense punishable under Section 21.

### 3. Concrete Recommendations for NCPCR Directives

We urge the National Commission for Protection of Child Rights to issue formal advisories and nationwide directives to all State Commissions (SCPCRs), Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE), and State Education Departments:

#### Directive 1: Standardized School SOP for AI-Enabled Sexual Bullying
- Issue an enforceable Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) instructing schools on how to respond when synthetic intimate imagery involving a student is discovered:
  1. **Immediate Crisis Support:** Provide trauma-informed counseling without interrogating, blaming, or suspending the victimized student.
  2. **Evidence Preservation:** Preserve digital screenshots, group chat identifiers, and file metadata securely without re-transmitting the illicit material.
  3. **Mandatory Reporting:** Mandate immediate reporting under Section 19 of POCSO to local police / SJPU and via the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (`cybercrime.gov.in`).
  4. **Fast-Track Takedown:** Assist parents in utilizing automated cryptographic takedown systems (such as *Take It Down* by NCMEC).

#### Directive 2: Accurate Helpline Routing and Awareness
- Ensure school safety boards prominently display correct, life-critical emergency numbers:
  - **1098:** Childline / Mission Vatsalya (24/7 emergency toll-free child protection).
  - **112:** All-India Emergency Police Assistance.
  - **181:** Women's Helpline (subject to applicable service availability).
  - *Clarification:* Clarify that national helpline **1930** is specifically designated for financial cyber fraud and should not be used in lieu of child emergency helplines.

#### Directive 3: Digital Literacy on Synthetic Identity Harm
- Integrate mandatory digital ethics and consent modules into secondary school curriculums, educating adolescents that creating, requesting, possessing, or forwarding synthetic intimate depictions of classmates is not a harmless prank, but a severe criminal offense under the POCSO Act and Indian Penal Code / Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita.
