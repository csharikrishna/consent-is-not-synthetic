### 1. The Emerging Crisis of Gendered Synthetic Abuse

Non-consensual synthetic intimate imagery—commonly referred to as "deepfake pornography" or "nudification"—is an overwhelmingly gendered form of digital violence. Global studies and operational hotline registries consistently demonstrate that over **95% of non-consensual deepfake media targets women and girls**, including journalists, students, professionals, and public figures.

The psychological and socio-economic consequences for survivors are profound and severe:
- Prolonged dignitary injury and reputational terror.
- Severe depressive trauma, panic disorders, and suicidal ideation.
- Social ostracization, familial alienation, and professional impairment.
- Coordinated extortion ("sextortion") demanding financial payment or real sexual favors under threat of viral dissemination.

### 2. Legal Protections Under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) & IT Act

Victims of synthetic intimate image abuse in India possess strong statutory remedies under existing criminal statutes:
1. **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023:**
   - **Section 79 (Word, gesture or act intended to insult modesty of a woman):** Criminalizes the creation, display, or transmission of any image or media intended to outrage or insult the modesty of a woman (punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years and fine).
   - **Section 351 (Criminal Intimidation):** Directly applies to sextortion demands threatening the publication of altered photographs.
   - **Section 78 (Stalking):** Encompasses monitoring a woman’s digital likeness or online presence to generate and circulate synthetic content without consent.
2. **Information Technology Act, 2000:**
   - **Section 66E (Violation of Privacy):** Captures the intentional capture, publishing, or transmitting of personal image representations without consent.
   - **Section 67 & 67A (Obscene & Sexually Explicit Acts):** Strict liability for circulating synthetic sexually explicit depictions (punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment and fine).
   - **Rule 3(2)(b) of IT Rules, 2021 (as amended):** Imposes a strict **2-hour takedown duty** on hosting intermediaries upon receiving a complaint from the affected woman or her authorized representative.

### 3. Actionable Recommendations for NCW Intervention

We formally urge the National Commission for Women to spearhead the following survivor-centric initiatives:

#### Recommendation 1: Dedicated Fast-Track NCII Redressal Cell
- Establish a specialized **Synthetic Image Abuse & Digital Dignity Desk** within the NCW 24/7 Cyber Cell (`+91-7827170170`), staffed by female cyber law officers trained in trauma-informed victim intake.
- Assist victims in issuing statutory 2-hour Section 79 takedown notices directly to intermediary grievance officers.

#### Recommendation 2: Partnerships with Global Cryptographic Hashing Systems
- Facilitate official partnership between NCW, MeitY, and non-profit cryptographic platforms such as **StopNCII.org** (administered by SWGfL) and **Take It Down** (NCMEC).
- These systems allow victims to create irreversible cryptographic hashes of their authentic images locally on their personal devices, enabling participating tech platforms to block synthetic swaps before dissemination without the victim ever sharing private photographs with anyone.

#### Recommendation 3: Legal Aid & Pro Bono Representation
- Form an empaneled roster of pro bono cyber-law advocates to assist female survivors in securing ex-parte judicial injunctions ("John Doe" / Ashok Kumar orders) restraining anonymous perpetrators and web hosts from disseminating synthetic content.
